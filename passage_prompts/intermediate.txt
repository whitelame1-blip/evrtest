STRICT PASSAGE GENERATION RULES (INTERMEDIATE LEVEL / PASSAGE 2 STYLE)
1. Topic Focus
The text must be non-specialized and informational, based on real-world topics familiar to a general audience.
The topic may involve social, scientific, historical, or environmental themes but must not require expert knowledge.
2. Content Length
The passage MUST be exactly {word_count} words long.
3. Language & Meaning
The writing must be clear but moderately sophisticated.
Avoid ambiguity, but allow implicit meaning that requires careful reading.
Main ideas should not always be stated in the same sentence as supporting details.
4. Vocabulary
Use intermediate-level vocabulary (B1+/B2).
Prefer paraphrasing and synonyms over repetition.
Avoid rare, technical, or academic terminology unless it is commonly known and clearly explained in context.
5. Sentence Structure
Use a mix of simple, compound, and controlled complex sentences.
Include relative clauses and passive voice sparingly and naturally.
Avoid overly long or heavily nested sentences.
6. Cohesion & Referencing
Use cohesive devices such as however, therefore, as a result, for example, in contrast.
Refer back to ideas using pronouns and paraphrased references rather than repeating key nouns.
7. Paragraph Structure
Organize the text into 3–4 logical paragraphs, each with:
A clear main idea
Supporting explanation or example
In the final JSON output, paragraphs must be joined into a single string using "\n\n" markers, not actual newlines.
8. Factual Accuracy
All information must be factually accurate and based on widely accepted knowledge.
No invented statistics, fictional studies, or speculative claims.
9. Reader Task Alignment
The passage should require the reader to:
Identify paraphrased information
Connect ideas across sentences
Distinguish main ideas from supporting details
Information should be locatable but not always word-for-word.
ANTI-REPETITION & PARAPHRASING RULES
Do NOT repeat the same sentence structure or phrasing more than once.
If a key idea must reappear, restate it using different vocabulary or grammatical form.
Avoid repeated sentence openers (e.g., “Many people believe…”).
Each paragraph must introduce at least one new perspective, cause, effect, or example.
DIFFICULTY INTEGRITY CHECK (MANDATORY)
Before finalizing the passage, verify:
Could a reader answer factual questions without direct sentence matching?
Are ideas restated using synonyms or rephrased structures?
